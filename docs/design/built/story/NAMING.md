# Naming rules

Up: [built story](README.md)

The rules for every name the player reads, in force now. A new name passes the lore check first: the [sotano-lore](../../../../.claude/skills/sotano-lore/SKILL.md) skill and its library, [SOURCES](../../../lore/SOURCES.md).

## Practices

- In-game names are generic technique or concept names ("Exposure", "Kaizen", "Box Breathing", "Deep Focus"), or titles of public-domain works (*Meditations*, *Self-Reliance*, *As a Man Thinketh*, *Tao Te Ching*).
- Modern books, courses and branded methods are credited only in the practice's inspiration line, never as its name. Some are registered trademarks (the Pomodoro Technique, Getting Things Done, the Wim Hof Method, *The 7 Habits*). They are never names.
- Each source has one of four statuses in the library: PD (public domain: the title may be a name), Concept (a generic technique: the generic name may be a name), Credit (a modern work: credit only), Caution (a registered trademark, a disputed right, or a term coined by its author: never a name, and its distinctive terms are avoided).
- Where a built name conflicts with these statuses, it is recorded as an open question in its practice document and listed in the [practices index](../practices/README.md#open-questions). No one renames a practice on their own.

## Projections, intentions and places

- Enemies are projections: patterns that block intention. They are never people, groups, diagnoses or traditions. A projection behaves like the pattern it stands for.
- The player's shot is an **intention**, in the code and in the text. Never "tear".
- No names, characters or assets from other companies or games are used. The game is open source and the names are generic or public-domain.
- Places take names from the story. Floors are numbered, and their circles carry the names of the descent (the HUD shows, for example, "Floor 3" with its circle's name).

## Good and bad intentions

The two intentions are good and bad, and only that. Everywhere the player can read them (menus, tooltips, toasts, the tutorial, these documents) the text says: *good intentions heal and turn enemies into allies; bad intentions break and destroy.* There are no abstract names for them: no gold, no ember, no positive, no negative in player text. The code keeps `positive` and `negative` as identifiers. The owner's rule is repeated in [VOCABULARY](VOCABULARY.md#polarity).

## Tone

- Respectful. Practices are not magic cures, and therapies are not jokes. Projections are patterns, not people.
- A practice's description says what the practice is about, in one line, in the story's voice. Its effect says what it does, plainly and with numbers. Both are shown on pickup and in the inventory.
- Every practice has a path tag, unless the owner says otherwise.

## Winks at other games

The game is a love letter to games. A reference to another game is welcome as a paraphrased line, a familiar mechanic or a situation a player of that game will smile at. It is never a copy: no names, characters, logos, assets, music, sprites, exact layouts, quotes longer than one line, or trademarks of another game.

- The wink must still mean something in the story. The reference comes after the metaphor, not instead of it. Lion's Mane is the model: a mushroom that makes you jump higher and break bricks with your head, and a toast now and then about a ceiling "in another castle". The mushroom is about growing past the ceiling you were told was the limit.
- Where a wink lives: a practice's messages (toasts), its description, its inspiration line ("a nod to"), room names and secrets.
- Each game is catalogued in [SOURCES, games](../../../lore/SOURCES.md#games), with what the game gives and what it never takes.

## Open naming questions

Ten practice names and two credits are open. They are listed with their codes in the [practices index](../practices/README.md#open-questions). Until the owner decides, the names stay as built.
